WHOLE-SCREEN TITLE / ENTRY-FLOW GUIDANCE
Research-based guidance for the supplied TIDELINE brief. This is additional guidance, not another product brief or a prescribed mockup. Fulfill every behavior and viewport in the common brief. Keep your design decisions yours. Transfer the mechanisms below as a coordinated screen system; merely changing the palette is not an improvement.

Observed composition: almost pure black full frame. Enormous centered two-tier textured title fills roughly x13–87%, y23–50%; compact centered menu is much lower, around y75–88%. The world/atmosphere is expressed here by the logo material and negative space, not a scenery card. Title letters have visible aged-metal/stone texture; small menu letters are pale serif capitals. Continue, Load Game, New Game, System are in a close vertical stack. Selected Continue rests on a long, thin ochre haze/glow that fades out at the edges. There is no bounded rounded rectangle, gold border, filled card stack, or bevel around each menu entry. A small downward triangle indicates more rows; online/version information is peripheral at lower right. These are approximate inspected image positions, not canonical implementation tokens. Only a static selected state was inspected; hover/press animation timing is unknown.
Entry facts from the publisher manual: Continue and Load Game cannot be selected on first play. Continue resumes last-session data; Load Game chooses a profile; New Game starts afresh; System changes settings. Thus save availability is part of main-menu hierarchy, not a label pasted over an always-active action.
Transferable mechanism: separate the game's identity scale from the menu's interaction scale; give one selected command a localized treatment and leave unselected commands materially quiet. Centering is appropriate to this particular symmetrical, empty backdrop. Its coordinates, serif choice, gold hue, and logo texture are not universal recipes.

B. DESIGN PROPOSALS FOR THIS PARTICULAR TIDELINE ART / BRIEF
The shared artwork already contains real spatial depth: large near-right dish/building, smaller receding station, distant sea/horizon, dark wet rocks, and a tiny explorer. Treat that as the screen's focal world, not as wallpaper behind a centered dashboard. Its quiet left third is the candidate text region. Unlike Nightreign's void, this art does not justify forcing a giant centered serif logo over the dish/horizon.

Whole frame and depth
- Preserve the large dish silhouette and tiny explorer, the horizon glow, and the near/far station scale relationship. Avoid a global opaque wash that destroys all three depth cues. Use only a localized, soft-edged contrast scrim beneath the text region if needed; it should disappear into the art rather than read as a rectangular panel.
- At desktop, try a single left content axis with a moderate upper-left title, a substantial breath before the main commands, and a small bottom input/help area. The central/right majority should remain uninterrupted art. Approximate starting ranges, to adjust optically: text axis 6–8% from left, title around upper 12–24%, command group around lower-middle 50–78%; never rely on those ranges when they create collisions.
- Do not add inventory grids, side-stat cards, dashboard charts, a marketing navigation bar, giant translucent modal chrome, unnecessary telemetry, or decorative panels to fill empty space. Give the world room to establish mood.

Typography, density, and alignment together
- Give TIDELINE a designed wordmark treatment using the available condensed bold sans if suitable: deliberate uppercase proportions and restrained tracking, with an optical weight/size that can hold against the art. Do not use thin widely spaced text as a substitute for title design, or imitate the Nightreign serif/stone logo. A tiny subtitle is optional; keep it to one short line.
- Use a highly legible regular/semibold sans for menu/body text, rather than copying the title face everywhere. Establish three clear scales: identity, commands, small support. The most important command needs visual priority, but extra-loud button decoration should not fight the title and scene.
- Align title, command labels, concise Continue-unavailable reason, and contextual help to one considered axis. Judge optical alignment of the letters, not only the outer button boxes. Keep the command column narrow enough that there is no large empty padded button rectangle.
- Main-screen text should be sparse: four requested commands, one short reason for unavailable Continue, and at most one short contextual/helper line. No explanatory paragraphs beside every entry. Preserve legibility on the bright horizon and textured waves; do not fix contrast by shrinking labels or fading functional text to nearly invisible gray.

Menu silhouette, surface, and states
- Choose a coherent environmental/sci-fi interaction language: chiefly text-led command rows, square or subtly cut/open-ended silhouettes, shallow translucent ink/surface support when needed, and one precise leading marker or rail. A full opaque rounded pill stack or olive/brass ornamental fantasy panel has no basis in these inspected references or this art.
- Unselected, hover, keyboard-focus, pressed, selected, and unavailable must be distinguishable. Color alone is insufficient. For example: rest = quiet readable label; hover/focus = brighter label plus visible leading marker/rail and localized row support; pressed = brief sharper/inset intensity change; disabled Continue = subdued yet readable label plus concise reason; durable difficulty selection = visible marker/check and persistently distinct support. Avoid scale jumps that make the column wobble.
- A row can have a generous invisible hit area while remaining visually lean. Aim for at least 44px touch height on small screens. Keep any material/grain/bloom subtle and localized. Generic outer drop shadows, bevels, huge glows, and outlines on every item do not create AAA craft. The shape, type, rhythm, and scene relationship must work even with the accent color removed.

Entry flow is the same visual system, not disconnected popups
- Initial menu must present New expedition as the available way in and make Continue visibly unavailable with its reason. Do not place Continue as an active-looking competing primary call to action with a late error toast.
- New expedition should advance into a clearly titled setup state with Story / Standard, Standard initially selected, and a deliberate Start expedition action plus Back. Retain the art/world and the existing content axis or another clearly related composition. Replace prior menu choices instead of piling multiple panels/dialogs on top. Do not claim to have saved until Start expedition actually succeeds.
- Start has a clear transition into the local survey scene. Reduce title/menu chrome there so it reads as play: selected difficulty, a compact actual objective, and Return to menu are enough. Use the session's state to restore the same scene/difficulty through Continue. Returning to the main menu should visibly explain the new availability, without inserting a competing third promotion/banner.
- Settings and Credits need the same type, surfaces, hierarchy, and Back/Close grammar. The volume value and Reduce motion checkbox must be legible controls, not tiny decorative footer icons. Preserve state through Back/Escape. Focus should go to the new view's heading/first relevant control and return predictably to its invoking command when closed.

Safe areas and narrow screens
- Keep functional content within real margins and safe-area insets. Footer hints should not touch screen edges or overlap the menu. Desktop whitespace is not permission to waste the short landscape height.
- Landscape phone: compress the identity/breath region first, retain four lean touchable rows and visible Back controls, and keep the dish/world visible. Do not merely scale the entire desktop composition until text becomes tiny.
- Portrait: the artwork's quiet left third will not survive an arbitrary center cover-crop. Deliberately choose a crop and contrast region or a scene-above / command-below composition that still feels like one game title screen. Keep the four commands and their reason readable without a rotate barrier. Prioritize a retained recognizable dish/coast silhouette and one coherent hierarchy, not the desktop coordinates.
- Reduced motion must suppress added drifting/parallax/long animations. Keep all information available without motion, hover, or audio.

C. HOW TO JUDGE THE RESULT
At a glance, can someone see a specific game world, a memorable title, and one obvious way into it? Is the dish/explorer still a focal subject? Does the art-to-UI proportion stay generous? Do title, menu type, silhouette, material, whitespace, and state grammar support one another? Does keyboard focus look as intentionally designed as pointer hover? Does unavailable Continue explain itself before activation? Can all specified entry/return/settings flows be completed in every viewport? More boxes, more colors, or a tiny panel reorder are not evidence of better design.
